These are suggestions, not statements of fact. They were written from the course content — not from watching you teach. An AI suggestion has no idea what you actually did this year, which tools your district approved, or which of these you've genuinely put into practice.

Resume-Ready Bullet Points

resume.txt — copy, then make it yours
01
Credential

Completed the AI-Ready Educator: Foundations & Impact certification, covering AI fundamentals, instructional applications, tool evaluation, and responsible use in [K–12 / higher ed / your setting].

True for you if: you finished the course. This is the safest bullet on the page — and the one most worth pairing with a second, more specific line.

02
Teaching & Learning

Used AI to differentiate materials for [subject/grade], producing the same content at multiple reading levels so [number] students could access grade-level ideas at an appropriate level of challenge.

Only if you've actually done it. If you've made one leveled text, say one — small and true beats large and vague.

03
Teaching & Learning

Cut lesson-planning time on [unit/course] by using AI to draft outlines, discussion prompts, and exit tickets, then revising each against [standards / my students' needs] before use.

The second half is the point. "Drafted with AI, revised by me" is the professional claim — not "AI made my lessons."

04
Teaching & Learning

Built a teacher-in-the-loop feedback workflow for [assignment type], using AI for first-pass structural comments while personally reviewing and approving every comment before it reached a student.

Strong because it names a safeguard. Say it only if you truly reviewed every one.

05
Communication

Improved family communication by using AI to draft and tone-check messages about [progress / behaviour / attendance], personalising each before sending and keeping student details out of unapproved tools.

The privacy clause is doing real work here. Include it — it signals you know the rule.

06
Communication

Expanded access for multilingual families by using AI-assisted translation for [newsletters / progress updates], with review before release to protect accuracy and tone.

Equity work, stated plainly. Adjust "review" to whatever you genuinely did — a colleague, a staff translator, your own read.

07
Tool Evaluation

Evaluated [number] AI tools against a structured framework — problem fit, level of teacher control, student-data safety, alignment to teaching values, and ease of adoption — and recommended [tool] to [my team / department].

This is the Five Questions framework from Module 1D. Powerful on a résumé because most educators can't describe a selection process at all.

08
Tool Evaluation

Piloted [tool] with [group] over [timeframe], evaluated its real classroom impact rather than its demo, and made a documented keep/drop recommendation.

"Rather than its demo" is the line interviewers remember. Only use it if you actually ran a pilot and reached a decision.

09
Ethics & Privacy

Applied student-data privacy practice to AI use, keeping personally identifiable information out of unvetted tools and working only within [district-approved platforms] in line with FERPA.

Say this only if it's your genuine habit. It's the one bullet a district will test you on.

10
Ethics & Privacy

Wrote a plain-language AI-use statement for [students / families] explaining what AI is used for, what it is never used for, and how student information is protected.

If you haven't written one, this is the easiest item here to make true — it takes an afternoon, and it's a real artefact you can attach.

11
Leadership

Led [a PLC session / department PD / grade-level meeting] on practical and responsible AI use, helping [number] colleagues evaluate tools and set their own classroom boundaries.

Only if you led something. "Shared what I learned with two colleagues" is also true and also worth writing — just write that instead.

12
Leadership

Contributed to [school/district] AI guidance by advising on [acceptable use / academic integrity / tool approval], representing classroom realities in the policy decision.

Strong for anyone moving toward instructional coaching or administration. Requires that you were genuinely consulted.

Make It Even Stronger

Three swaps that beat any generic bullet

1
Name the setting. "7th-grade science," not "the classroom." Specific is believable.
2
Add a number. Students reached, hours saved per week, tools compared, colleagues trained. Even a small honest number outperforms an adjective.
3
Say what changed. Not what you used — what was different afterwards for students, families, or your team.
Generic

Used AI to differentiate materials for students.

⚡ Stronger — and checkable

Differentiated a 7th-grade science unit with AI, producing readings at three levels so all 28 students worked from the same core content — cutting prep from roughly three hours to forty minutes.
